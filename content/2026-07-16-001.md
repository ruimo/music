# Liner Notes

## Edvard Grieg: Lyric Pieces, Op. 68, No. 2
### *Grandmother’s Minuet* (*Bestemors menuett*)

Edvard Grieg’s ten-volume *Lyric Pieces* (*Lyriske stykker*) serve as an intimate musical diary, capturing the landscapes, folklore, and domestic life of his beloved Norway. Among these miniature masterpieces, **"Grandmother’s Minuet"** (Op. 68, No. 2), composed in 1898, stands out as a delightfully whimsical and nostalgic gem.

---

### The Character and Appeal

* **A Sprightly Portrait:** 
  Rather than depicting a frail, sedentary elder, Grieg paints a portrait of a grandmother filled with life, warmth, and a mischievous twinkle in her eye. She is coaxed onto the dance floor, recalling the steps of her youth with effortless grace.

* **Rococo Elegance Meets Nordic Wit:** 
  The piece is a brilliant pastiche of the classical 18th-century minuet. Grieg masterfully blends the courtly, delicate poise of the Rococo era with his signature Norwegian harmonic shifts and rhythmic vitality.

* **Playful Articulation:** 
  The charm of the piece lies in its articulation. It features crisp, tiptoeing staccatos, sudden dynamic contrasts, and playful grace notes (acciaccaturas) that mimic the rustle of a silk skirt or a sudden, knowing wink. 

* **Nostalgic Middle Section:** 
  A brief, lyrical contrast in the middle section offers a moment of tender reflection—perhaps a fleeting memory of a bygone romance—before returning to the sprightly, mock-formal dance theme.

---

### Why It Resonates

At just under two minutes, "Grandmother's Minuet" is a masterclass in musical economy. Grieg evokes a vivid character, a specific dance tradition, and a poignant sense of passing time, all within a deceptively simple piano miniature. It remains an enchanting favorite for listeners and pianists alike, celebrated for its lightness of touch and heartwarming humor.
