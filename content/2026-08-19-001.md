### Franz Liszt: *Liebestraum* No. 3 in A-flat major, S. 541
#### **“O lieb, so lang du lieben kannst” (Love as long as you can love)**

Franz Liszt’s *Liebestraum* (Love Dream) No. 3 is arguably the most famous love song ever written for the piano. While recognized globally as a solo piano masterpiece, its origins lie in a set of three lieder (songs) Liszt composed for voice and piano, set to poetry by Ferdinand Freiligrath. The poem’s message is both beautiful and haunting: a plea to love unconditionally and passionately, for the hour will inevitably come when we must mourn at a loved one's grave.

##### **The Music: From Poetic Song to Keyboard Masterpiece**

In this solo piano transcription, Liszt strips away the spoken word but amplifies the emotional core of the poem through the expressive voice of the piano. 

* **The Melodic Thread:** The piece opens with a tender, song-like melody in the middle register, enveloped by a gentle wave of A-flat major arpeggios. Liszt cleverly divides the accompaniment between both hands, creating a seamless, floating texture that mimics a singer's breath.
* **The Cadenzas:** Twice during the piece, the melody pauses for Liszt’s signature cadenzas. Far from empty virtuosity, these delicate, cascading runs descend like stardust, shifting the key and transitioning the listener into new emotional landscapes.
* **The Ecstatic Climax:** As the work progresses, the quiet longing builds into a surge of passion. The melody is transformed into soaring, triumphant octaves, supported by a rich, stormy bass, pushing the acoustic limits of the instrument.
* **The Serene Resolution:** The storm subsides into a state of blissful peace. The opening theme returns, now whisper-soft, leaving the listener in a dreamlike state of quiet contemplation.

##### **Why It Endures**

What makes *Liebestraum* No. 3 an eternal favorite is Liszt’s ability to balance dazzling pianistic brilliance with profound, universal sentiment. It is a work of dualities: technically demanding yet effortlessly fluid; deeply melancholic yet radiating hope. In this single movement, Liszt captures the entire spectrum of human affection—from the first quiet spark of romance to the fiery heights of passion, and finally, to the timeless nature of devotion.
