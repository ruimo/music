# Edvard Grieg: "Watchman's Song" (*Vektersang*), Op. 12, No. 3
### From *Lyric Pieces*, Book I (1867)

Edvard Grieg’s *Lyric Pieces* serve as an intimate musical diary, spanning 66 miniature masterpieces across ten books. Among these, **"Watchman’s Song" (Op. 12, No. 3)** stands out as one of his earliest and most theatrical creations—a haunting nocturnal drama captured in just a few minutes.

#### The Inspiration: A Shakespearean Chill
The piece was inspired by a performance of Shakespeare’s *Macbeth* that Grieg attended in Copenhagen. Captivated by the dark, supernatural atmosphere of the play and the traditional role of the night watchman, Grieg set out to paint a vivid picture of the midnight hour.

#### A Musical Journey in Three Acts
The piece is structured in a stark, dramatic ternary (A-B-A) form:

*   **The Reassurance (A Section):** 
    The piece opens with a solemn, hymn-like melody in E major. Written in a steady, triple meter, it evokes the heavy footsteps of the watchman on his nightly rounds. His song is warm, comforting, and chorale-like, offering a sense of security to the sleeping town.

*   **The Haunting (B Section - Intermezzo):** 
    Without warning, the atmosphere shifts from comfort to terror. Marked *poco piu vivo*, this section represents the "spirits of the night." Grieg utilizes eerie, rapid tremolos and whispering staccato chords in the upper register, contrasted with dark, rumbling octaves in the bass. It is a psychological shudder—a sudden confrontation with the supernatural.

*   **The Return to Light (A Section & Coda):** 
    The watchman’s chorale returns, dispelling the ghostly illusions. The piece closes with a poignant coda, where the main theme gently dissolves into the quiet of the night, leaving the listener with a lingering sense of peace and mystery.

#### Why It Captivates
"Watchman’s Song" is a masterclass in economy of scale. With remarkable simplicity, Grieg contrasts light and shadow, the human and the supernatural, the comforting and the uncanny. It remains a beloved gem of the romantic piano repertoire, proving that a composer does not need a massive orchestra to tell a thrilling story.
