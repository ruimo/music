# Liner Notes: Edvard Grieg – Lyric Pieces, Op. 65: No. 2, "Peasant's Song"

Edvard Grieg was the undisputed master of the musical miniature, and his ten volumes of *Lyric Pieces* (*Lyriske stykker*) serve as an intimate diary of his musical soul. Among these, **"Peasant's Song" (Bondens sang)**, the second piece from his Op. 65 collection published in 1897, stands out as a poignant tribute to the spirit of the Norwegian countryside.

### The Essence of the Norwegian Soul

While some of Grieg’s rustic pieces burst with the kinetic energy of peasant dances, "Peasant's Song" is a work of quiet, introspective beauty. It is a heartfelt portrait of the Norwegian laborer—not painted with broad, sweeping strokes, but with the delicate, warm colors of a home-bound evening. 

### Musical Characteristics & Appeal

* **Hymn-like Simplicity:** The piece opens with a simple, chorale-like melody. Its homophonic texture and steady, rocking rhythm evoke a sense of solemnity, peace, and deep-seated dignity.
* **Folk-Inflected Harmonies:** Grieg masterfully infuses classical harmony with the unique modal colors of Norwegian folk music. Subtle shifts in harmony capture the natural majesty of the Nordic landscape—its fjords, mountains, and twilight shadows.
* **Expressive Contrast:** The musicebbs and flows between tender vulnerability and moments of rich, resonant strength. Grieg utilizes the piano's middle register to mimic the warm resonance of a male choir or a solo vocal lament.

### Why It Resonates

"Peasant's Song" appeals directly to the heart. It does not rely on virtuosic display; instead, its power lies in its utter sincerity and nostalgia. In just a few minutes, Grieg transports the listener to a quiet wooden cottage in the late Norwegian summer, offering a moment of pure, unpretentious solace that is as comforting today as it was over a century ago.
