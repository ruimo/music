# Franz Liszt: Étude in A Minor, S. 137, No. 2 (Molto vivace)

### The Genesis of a Virtuoso

Before Franz Liszt became the legendary keyboard wizard of the Romantic era, he was a teenage prodigy bursting with creative ambition. Composed in 1826 when Liszt was just fifteen years old, the *12 Études, Op. 1* (cataloged as S. 137) serve as the fascinating, youthful blueprint for what would eventually evolve into the monumental *Transcendental Études* (S. 139). Among these early gems, the **Étude No. 2 in A Minor (Molto vivace)** shines as a thrilling testament to Liszt’s budding genius.

### Youthful Fire and Classical Precision

While the later revisions of this étude are famous for their chaotic, hand-crossing leaps and dense textures, this original S. 137 version offers a captivating blend of Classical elegance and Romantic fire. Heavily influenced by his teacher, Carl Czerny, the young Liszt crafts a piece that is as much a display of finger independence as it is a poetic musical statement.

* **The Drive:** True to its marking, *Molto vivace* (Very lively), the piece takes off with a relentless, driving rhythm. A perpetual motion of rapid-fire notes demands absolute clarity and crisp articulation from the pianist.
* **The Texture:** Unlike the heavy, orchestrally-conceived works of his maturity, this étude sparkles with a transparent, pearlescent texture. The melody is woven directly into the fast-flowing figuration, requiring the performer to sing through a whirlwind of keys.
* **The Drama:** Written in the turbulent key of A minor, the piece carries an urgent, dramatic undercurrent. Brief shifts into major keys offer fleeting moments of light before the relentless momentum sweeps the listener back into the shadows.

### Why It Captivates

The appeal of S. 137, No. 2 lies in its raw, unfiltered energy. It provides a rare window into the mind of a young Liszt—not yet the theatrical showman, but a brilliant young artist exploring the limits of his instrument. Listening to this piece is like watching the first sparks of a fire that would soon set the entire musical world ablaze. It is short, breathless, and utterly exhilarating.
