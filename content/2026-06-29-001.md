# Liner Notes: Chopin’s Mazurka in E-Flat Minor, Op. 6, No. 4

## A Fleeting Shadow in the Night

Frédéric Chopin’s Mazurkas represent the very heartbeat of his homeland, Poland, distilled into the intimate world of the piano. Among his earliest published sets, the **Opus 6 Mazurkas** (published in 1832) served as his calling card to the salons of Paris, blending rustic folk origins with aristocratic sophistication. 

The final piece of this set, **No. 4 in E-Flat Minor**, is a stunning miniature of breathtaking brevity and dark, feverish intensity. 

---

### Key Features & Musical Appeal

* **A Whirlwind of Melancholy:** 
  Clocking in at under a minute in performance, this *Presto* mazurka is a masterclass in musical economy. It does not linger; instead, it sweeps past the listener like a sudden, chilly gust of wind.

* **The Dusk of E-Flat Minor:** 
  The choice of E-flat minor—a key Chopin often reserved for his most introspective, somber, and turbulent thoughts—shrouds the piece in a shadowy, twilight atmosphere. 

* **Restless Rhythm:** 
  Unlike the more leisurely, danceable mazurkas in the set, No. 4 relies on a breathless, perpetual motion (*moto perpetuo*) drive. The characteristic triple-meter accents are whipped into a frantic, dizzying spin.

* **Chromatic Intricacy:** 
  Beneath its rapid surface lies a sophisticated chromatic web. Chopin uses slipping, sliding harmonies that feel remarkably modern, creating a sense of emotional instability and unresolved yearning.

---

### Why It Captivates

Opus 6, No. 4 is the ultimate proof of Chopin’s genius in miniature form. It demands absolute precision and a delicate touch from the pianist, who must convey a lifetime of tragic longing in mere seconds. It is a haunting, elusive whisper—gone almost before you can fully grasp its beauty, leaving behind a lingering, unforgettable mystery.
