# Liner Notes: Liszt – Etude in C Major, S. 137: No. 1. Presto

### The Seed of Transcendence
Before Franz Liszt redefined the boundaries of piano technique with his monumental *Transcendental Études*, there was **S. 137**—the *Étude en douze exercices* (Study in Twelve Exercises). Composed in 1826 when Liszt was a mere fifteen-year-old prodigy, this collection offers a fascinating window into the genesis of a keyboard titan. 

### A Breathless Curtain-Raiser
The set opens with **No. 1 in C Major (*Presto*)**, a brilliant, lightning-fast miniature that serves as a thrilling call to arms. Though brief, it packs an extraordinary amount of momentum into its short duration:

* **Youthful Spark:** Strongly influenced by his teacher, Carl Czerny, the piece possesses a classical clarity and precision. Yet, underneath the academic discipline lies the unmistakable, restless spirit of the young Liszt.
* **Technical Spark:** Built upon rapid, cascading C-major scales and sweeping arpeggios, the etude demands fluid finger independence and absolute rhythmic control. 
* **Dramatic Urgency:** Rather than a dry finger exercise, Liszt infuses the music with a sense of theatrical flair, transforming basic scale patterns into a rush of pure sonic energy.

### Why It Captivates
The appeal of this *Presto* lies in its breathless, concentrated excitement. It is a musical snapshot of a young genius on the cusp of revolutionizing classical music. Serving as a sparkling burst of light, this etude does not just show off fingers of steel—it clears the air and sets the stage for the dramatic journey that lies ahead.
