# Liner Notes: Chopin’s Piano Sonata No. 2 in B-flat minor, Op. 35 — II. Scherzo

Nestled between the breathless drama of the opening movement and the solemn gravity of the famous *Funeral March*, the second movement of Frédéric Chopin’s Piano Sonata No. 2 is a tour de force of emotional extremes. Far from the traditional "joke" that the word *scherzo* originally implied, Chopin’s Scherzo in E-flat minor is a dark, thrilling, and tempestuous roller coaster.

### The Storm: Molto vivace
The movement erupts with an explosive, driving energy. Marked *Molto vivace*, it propels the listener forward with a relentless, pounding rhythm and menacing, chromatic textures. Chopin demands absolute virtuosic precision here; the pianist must navigate wide-leaping octaves and jagged, heavy chords with lightning speed. The mood is one of fierce defiance and manic urgency—a swirling vortex of sound that showcases Chopin’s ability to conjure orchestral power from a single instrument.

### The Oasis: Più lento
Just as the fury reaches a boiling point, Chopin guides us into one of his most sublime creations: the Trio section in G-flat major. The contrast is breathtaking. The tempest instantly subsides into a serene, song-like melody of heartbreaking beauty. It is an oasis of calm, reminiscent of Chopin's finest Nocturnes, where the piano sings with a warm, intimate, and deeply nostalgic voice. 

### The Resolution
The demonic main theme inevitably returns, reclaiming the narrative with its original fury. Yet, Chopin chooses not to end this battle with a chaotic explosion. Instead, the movement closes with a haunting whisper. The lyrical melody of the Trio echoes one last time, dissolving into a quiet, suspenseful chord that leaves the listener suspended over an abyss—perfectly setting the stage for the tragedy of the oncoming *Funeral March*.

### Why It Captivates
This Scherzo is a masterclass in dramatic juxtaposition. By placing terrifying violence alongside fragile, poetic beauty, Chopin captures the very essence of Romanticism. It remains a thrilling favorite for audiences and a supreme test of both technical bravura and emotional sensitivity for the performer.
