# Franz Liszt: Étude in A-Flat Major, S. 137, No. 9 *Andantino*

### The Seeds of Romantic Remembrance

Before Franz Liszt became the ultimate keyboard wizard of the 19th century, he was a fifteen-year-old prodigy finding his creative voice. Published in 1826, his *Étude en douze exercices*, S. 137, represents the earliest blueprint of what would eventually evolve into the monumental *Transcendental Études* (S. 139). Among these youthful creations, the **Ninth Étude in A-Flat Major (*Andantino*)** stands out as a breathtaking window into the composer’s developing lyricism. 

This piece is the direct ancestor of the famous, nostalgic masterwork *Ricordanza* ("Remembrance"). Hearing it in this early form is like discovering the elegant charcoal sketch that preceded a massive oil painting.

### Musical Style and Character

Unlike the thunderous octaves and lightning-fast scales usually associated with Liszt, this *Andantino* is a masterclass in delicacy, touch, and poetic expression:

* **Bel Canto Melody:** The piece centers on a tender, singing melody (*cantabile*) that floats gracefully over a fluid, undulating arpeggio accompaniment. It showcases Liszt's innate gift for writing vocal-like lines for the piano.
* **Warmth of A-Flat Major:** Written in the key of A-flat major—historically associated with warmth, love, and dreamlike states—the piece envelops the listener in a gentle, comforting acoustic glow.
* **Youthful Simplicity:** What makes this early version so captivating is its exquisite economy. Lacking the dense, hyper-virtuosic ornamentation of Liszt's later revisions, the pure, unadorned beauty of the melody takes center stage.

### Why It Captivates

The appeal of the *Andantino* lies in its intimacy. It is a quiet, reflective moment of repose that foreshadows the great Romantic nocturnes. Rather than overwhelming the listener with technical fireworks, the young Liszt coaxes a spectrum of colors from the keyboard through subtle shifts in harmony and phrasing. 

For the modern listener, this étude offers a rare and charming glimpse into the mind of a young genius—a delicate musical seed that would eventually blossom into one of the greatest masterpieces of the piano literature.
